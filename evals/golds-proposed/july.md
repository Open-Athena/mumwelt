# Marin's July 2026 plan

## The one-line milestone — and the fact that it is written in placeholders

The July milestone reads verbatim at the top of the weekly summaries as: **"complete 67B‑A2B MoE; start XB‑AYB MoE on B200s; start post‑training Marin MoEs"** ([summary 2026‑07‑06_2026‑07‑12](https://mws.oa.dev/summaries/summary-2026-07-06_2026-07-12.html)). Two things must be said before any of the numbers:

1. **July is a preparation month, not a finish line.** The summary is explicit: *"The July milestone runs to mid‑August and is a preparation month, not a finish line."* None of its three headline deliverables actually *completes* inside July — the 67B run lands ~Aug 30, the B200 run only *starts* at end of month, and post‑training only *starts* against intermediate cuts.

2. **The model sizes are narrator placeholders / weekly‑summary round numbers, not preregistered specs.** The milestone's own name is literally `XB‑AYB`. The end‑of‑month B200 run's issue [#6689](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6689) is titled **"[Hero Run] nB‑AmB XT on B200s"** — its body is a filled‑with‑`?` template ("Architecture & scaling recipe: MoE …"; FLOP budget `?`; Tokens `?`) that fixes only the *hardware* (288 B200 = 4×NVL72 on CoreWeave; "the last run before we train on the full **800+ B200 cluster**") and the *strategic slot* ("on the path to our **EOY 256–500B‑AYB run**"). The specific sizes **"~120B‑A8B"** (end‑of‑month B200 run), **"~512B‑A16B / 20T"** (September hero run) and **"XB‑AYB"** all originate in the **prose of the July 6–12 summary** — the summariser writes them with tildes ("~512B‑A16B", "~120B‑A8B"), flagging them as approximate — and appear in **no issue body**. As of the frozen week the actual shape of the Aug‑1 run ([#7073](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/7073)) is **open at 4/14 sub‑issues closed with "no decision recorded this week."** So every headline size in this plan should be read as an *approximate working figure*, not a committed number.

## What July is preparing *for*

The throughline for the rest of 2026 is the **Marin 2026 contender** hero run: when the **twelve NVL72 (B200) racks land around September 15**, the team kicks off its largest run — **a ~512B‑A16B model on 20T tokens** (summary prose; not preregistered). July's job is to be *ready* for that September run, and for the **~120B‑A8B B200 run that kicks off on 2 NVL72 at end of month**, by: finishing/cooling down the two in‑flight pretraining runs, getting Blackwell MFU up, locking data + architecture + a preregistered loss target, and pricing post‑training ([summary](https://mws.oa.dev/summaries/summary-2026-07-06_2026-07-12.html)).

The one genuinely **decided** architecture element for the September run is the attention: **grouped‑query attention (GQA)**, not MLA. Larry Dial's FLOP accounting in [#6522](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6522) shows MLA adds **+17.4% training FLOPs at 4k seqlen (+19–30% at 8k)** while its KV‑cache win is mostly an inference benefit, and he flagged the risk of standing up a new MLA implementation on the JAX/XLA GPU stack before Sept 15 — so "the working call is GQA."

## Four workstreams (the structure of the plan)

The month is organized as four named workstreams ([summary](https://mws.oa.dev/summaries/summary-2026-07-06_2026-07-12.html)):

1. **NVL72 (B200) pretraining MFU toward 25%** for the September run.
2. **A pretraining recipe that sets up post‑training well** — motivated by the pre‑to‑post feedback loop (below).
3. **Post‑training infrastructure that is fast enough** (and a working definition of "fast enough").
4. **A post‑training recipe** for the best model the available FLOPs allow.

These are carried on two structural tracks — **hero runs** (the concrete use of compute) and **commitments** (what must land to be ready) — with the bulk of the actual effort in **areas of investment** underneath.

**#6706's real body is "Get B200 MFUs above X in advance of July run / Need to be at 20%+?"** — the 20% is literally posed as a *question* with an `X` placeholder; the summary is the one that firms it to "20% … Aug 1 run." **#6710** ("B200 training MFU & perf") is explicitly *"Blocked by / after: Commitment #6706"* and carries the further 25% September target.

There are **two different B200 MFU figures this week and neither clears 20%:**
- **Single‑node 8×B200 = 17.8% MFU at width d5120** (achieved), via **Will Held wiring Tri Dao's QuACK SM100 grouped GEMM (the SonicMoE kernel)** into the expert MLPs ([#7012](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/7012)). - **Multinode JaxPP pipeline‑parallel = mean MFU 18.26 at ~414k tok/s** (achieved), on David Hall's `std_1f1b` JaxPP path across four 8×H100 nodes ([#7024](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/7024)) — **1.74 points below the 20% bar.** This came after a *disciplined week of negative results* (latency‑hiding scheduler flag +0.4% within noise; XLA ring combine −52%; input‑gradient‑first backward split −29%; etc.), each gated on A/B evidence; the most promising EP‑local QuACK adapter cleared the perf gate but failed output parity (a ~2.6% mismatch pinned to QuACK's fused approximate SwiGLU). David Hall's read: **"Hopper is back on the menu."**

So the commitment gating the ~120B‑A8B launch (**B200 MFU ≥ 20%, #6706**) is **unmet** as of the freeze — 17.8% single‑node, 18.26 multinode.

### Price post‑training ([#7074](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/7074))

Benjamin Feuer filled in the estimate (a **projection**, not a measurement). The pipeline is **RL over 10 experts, THEN multi‑teacher on‑policy distillation (MOPD) into the student on the Nemotron path** — *dominated by the RL leg*, whose cost scales with the experts' hosting footprint. Costed in **hardware FLOPs** (the pretraining plan's currency, so RL's **low ~1% aggregate MFU is *counted*, not hidden** — this is what inflates the HW‑FLOP price). Headline:
- **~0.25–0.9× the 10T pretrain in HW‑FLOPs** (≈ **1.2–3.9× the cheaper 2T cut**);
- only **~5–20% of the 120B big run**;
- a **~5× environment speedup divides all of it by five**;
- in literal tokens it is small — **~10⁹–10¹⁰, under 0.5% of a 10T pretrain** — though that undercounts, since each post‑training token is far costlier to produce.

**Empirical tension to flag:** the only executed RL‑vs‑OPD head‑to‑head in corpus ([#6218](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6218), penfever, Leonardo A100) found **pure RL beats OPD on accuracy *and* compute** for a small solvable task (Qwen3‑1.7B student: RL ~0.94–0.97 pass@8 at ~19–50 s/step vs OPD ~0.82–0.83 at 387–836 s/step; "OPD is teacher‑score‑bound — 90–97% of every step is the 32B teacher scoring"). Whether MOPD (multi‑teacher, post‑RL checkpoints, the plan's second leg) pays off at 67B/120B scale is unvalidated. The broader EOY plan is tracked in [#6053](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6053) (still multi‑document — a "straw plan" from Isaac Hodes/David Hall and a separate post‑training plan from penfever); the 67B‑A2B post‑training run itself is [#6705](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6705) (owner @penfever, "won't begin until early (or mid?) August").

### Pretraining data + tokenizer — the inputs that gate the data‑lock ([#7073](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/7073)); heavy on *negative* results

The summary's own section title is blunt: **"SuperBPE tokenizer edge reverses at scale; curation win isn't Pareto‑dominant."** Three sobering realities, not just the one win:

- **Tokenizer decision ([#6796](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6796), Russell Power, CLOSED):** a grug‑MoE bake‑off on **feBPB** (FLOP‑equivalent BPB pricing each tokenizer's serving cost at the ~250B‑total/~20B‑active deployment MoE). SuperBPE first read as 5/7 arms beating Llama‑3 at 6e19, but **the win is budget‑dependent and reverses at scale** — the **128k SuperBPE arms start level with Llama‑3 and fall progressively further behind as budget grows (gap roughly doubling out to 2.6e20)**, leaving only the trained 64k‑fixed arm ahead and only at low budget. **The decision moved off compression entirely: Will Held's GPT‑OSS tokenizer proposal** on ecosystem grounds (easier pass@K forecasting, teacher‑student alignment for OPD; Rohith Kuditipudi: ~98% of Llama tokens have direct Qwen counterparts for regular text). Related: [#5842](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5842) (Marin‑vs‑Qwen3 coverage) **closed completed**; [#6571](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6571) digit‑variant sweep **stuck in draft** (wrong model size gave the digit arms an unfair ~0.03 bpb edge).
- **Curation reality check ([#2351](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/2351), Michael Ryan / XenonMolecule) — the largest omission:** at **compute‑matched DCLM CORE eval, Marin's `high_quality` curation is NOT Pareto‑dominant even at the 3k scale** — the earlier apparent win relied on scale and had been hidden by scoring only at each run's minimum uncheatable‑eval val‑loss point. Gap analysis: strong on code, on par at math/Wikipedia, **real gaps in fiction, QA benchmarks, and news.** Remediation: a 935‑document dev set (keep/weak‑keep/weak‑drop/drop) drawn by cross‑pipeline disagreement, stratified by register.
- **The datakit quality/dedup/decontam defect wall (listed directly under #7073):** ~a dozen coordinated defect issues indicting the deployed fastText quality classifier and the domain/dedup/decontam stack — [#6849](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6849) (sorts by domain, not intrinsic quality), [#6850](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6850) (code collapses into one cluster), [#6851](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6851) (over‑merges templated synthetic data), [#6852](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6852) (decontam low‑recall on short‑line/embedded contamination), [#6854](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6854) (whole‑source dedup wipeouts, median ~76%/source), [#6855](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6855), [#6858](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6858) (over‑sharded, ~1 element/shard), [#6859](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6859), [#6860](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6860), plus July‑10 additions [#7124](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/7124) and [#7126](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/7126).

### Data‑selection diagnostics ([#6712](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6712)) — Rafal Wojdyla; and the pre→post loop

- **The one clean win: content‑embedding mixture surrogate** ([#6969](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6969), `mixing_via_embeddings`). It re‑featurizes a mixture as the token‑weighted histogram it induces over a frozen embedding codebook (`h = V·w`), fixing the structural weakness that mixture evidence is **bucket‑indexed** ([#7067](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/7067)). Preregistered validation passed, then a **live test priced a genuinely never‑swept bucket (`dolma_starcoder`) and realized 0.9410 uncheatable‑eval BPB** vs olmix‑reuse 0.9495, token‑proportional 0.9759, and the sweep's own best‑ever 0.9554 — a **−0.0145 margin at ~8σ** for one embedding job + minutes of CPU, no re‑sweep. **Preregistered caveat (do not drop it): winner's curse — the optimized point was optimistic by ~0.031 BPB**, so a trust‑region/LCB proposer is the flagged next step. Note the honest tension: the win is on a *code* bucket, exactly where curation is already strong and the quality classifiers are most broken.
- **The strategic pre→post feedback loop ([#6096](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6096)):** Isaac Hodes raised it for the team agenda; Rafal Wojdyla distilled it to two questions (what should pretraining hill‑climb to make post‑training easier; how should post‑training feed back into pretraining data). **Will Held sketched redefining "compute‑optimal" for an RL world — fit the hero‑run scaling ladder to pass@256 on agent‑relevant skills** (on the assumption pass@256 converts to pass@1 under enough RL), which lets overtraining be priced against the RL‑cost savings it buys. This is what motivates workstream 2 ("a pretraining recipe that sets up post‑training well"). No experiment has closed the loop yet.
