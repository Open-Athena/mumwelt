# Our Muon approach, and how we got here

## Short answer

Our production optimizer is **MuonH** — Muon (momentum → **Newton-Schulz orthogonalization** of each 2-D update) fitted with its *own* AdamH-style learning-rate scaling law and wrapped in a **Hyperball / Frobenius-hyperball** constraint on the matrix parameters. It is the optimizer baked into the **"May Recipe"** that merged to `main` in [PR #6153](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/6153) (ClassicLarry, 2026-06-04), and it is what the live **67B-A2B Grug MoE hero run** trains with on **TPU v4-2048** ([#6044](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6044)). MuonH replaces AdamH on the matrix-shaped ("weight-matrix") parameter group; the lm-head / output projection, token embeddings, routers, router biases, and vector/scalar leaves stay on Adam/AdamH.

We got here through a March→June bake-off on the MoE path ([#4034](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4034) → [#5167](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5167) → [#5596](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5596)), a per-recipe LR refit ([#5951](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5951)), an isoFLOP compute-optimal study ([#6074](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6074)), and a **hardware split**: Muon is essentially free on TPU (XLA hides the Newton-Schulz cost) but a large fixed tax on GPU/FSDP ([#6493](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6493)), so the TPU recipe keeps Muon while the parallel GPU/Blackwell track has *dropped* it for now.

## 1. What the current approach actually is

**MuonH = Muon + a dedicated LR heuristic + a Hyperball constraint.** The optimizer thread that produced it set out to "define a provisional MuonH scaling heuristic analogous to the existing AdamH heuristic" and shipped a `MoeMuonHHeuristic` config; it closed *"we went with MuonH here"* (ClassicLarry, 2026-06-06, [#5167](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5167)). Under the hood it is still standard Muon — momentum, then Newton-Schulz orthogonalization of the 2-D update — a lineage (`MuonConfig`/`MuonHConfig`/`GrugMuonConfig`) first proven on dense LLaMA/Qwen ([#4034](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4034), claude[bot] research note, 2026-03-23). At hero scale the matrix machinery is load-bearing for *stability*: Larry root-caused a v4-2048 instability to "the sharded NS [Newton-Schulz] calc in Muon … silently dropping when num_matrices didn't evenly divide num_chips" (256×26 divides 512 chips but not 1024), fixed by replicating the NS calc over the replica axis ([#6044](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6044), 2026-06-27).

**Parameter routing (as shipped in the May Recipe).**
- MuonH covers the matrix group — attention matmuls, MoE expert matmuls, the shared expert, and GatedNorms — via a scale-invariant Newton-Schulz + Frobenius-hyperball update; **only lm_head / output_proj stays on AdamH** ([#6044](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6044) job summary; [#5596](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5596)).
- Two routing ablations settled the edges: an attempt to move attention **K,V to AdamH regressed** (**+0.0140 loss at d512**, "Muon isn't actually helping on the KV projections"), so attention K/V stay on MuonH (ClassicLarry, [#5719](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5719), 2026-05-15); the **GatedNorm-gains-to-AdamH** stack was tuned in the same cluster ([#5645](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5645), [#5719](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5719)).
- Expert `w_gate` / `w_up` are stored **split** (`split_moe_gate_up_for_ortho`) so each is orthogonalized as its own leaf and concatenated only inside the forward ([#5167](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5167), [#6153](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/6153)).

**Its own LR law.** MuonH gets a dedicated law `LR = m · tokens^(−a) · hidden_dim^(−b) · batch_size^0.5`, refit for the May Recipe in [#5951](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5951) (ClassicLarry, 2026-05-26). The committed `heuristic.py` change reads **`lr_coeff` 0.02547 → 0.09700**, **`lr_tokens_exp` −0.2813 → −0.395** (R²=0.996 on the refit). *Fidelity caveat:* those are mid-thread values from a **self-correction cascade** — Larry flagged the 60% LR jump as "surprising," then posted a *"Correction: previous fit had a units bug"* (the fit script used `intermediate_dim = d·4` vs the actual `≈ d/2`), and on 2026-06-05 re-plotted after normalizing for batch size ("the chart above is a bit deceptive"). R²=0.996 is fit quality, not confirmation the post-correction shipped constants exactly match these figures; treat the exponent/coefficient magnitudes as the committed direction (a ~60% LR increase over the v16 sweep), not audited final digits.

**The live hero-run config** it trains ([#6044](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6044) job summary, 2026-06-27): d=2560, 67.1B total / **2.01B active per token** (2.34B with lm_head), 26 layers, **256 experts K=4**, GQA 4:1, seqlen 8192, sliding-window 2048, ~**10.07T tokens (~1.2e23 FLOPs)**, MuonH on **v4-2048 us-central2**. Recipe knobs baked in: **PKO disabled**, **RoPE removed on global layers**, **final-logit z-loss re-added**, **LR decays to 5% of peak, not 0** (for planned context-extension + posttraining), with a **batch ramp 4,096 → 8,192 at step 15,000**.

## 2. What it buys — labeled achieved / measured / projected

There are two distinct speedup numbers in the corpus; they must not be conflated.

**(a) Measured, optimizer-only wall-clock speedup (the load-bearing argument).** In the gate-1/gate-2 matrix-swap study, MuonH beat the v16 AdamH baseline on Paloma macro_loss at every measured scale with a **measured wall-clock speedup that shrinks monotonically with scale: d512 1.33×, d768 1.26×, d1024 1.22×, d1280 1.19×** (WhenWen, gate-2 close-out, [#5596](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5596), 2026-05-11). Kaiyue Wen's read: "current speedup on the scale we tested is 30–40%… projected to 1e23 our prediction shrinks to 4%… our Muon implementation's overhead is smaller than that already (1–2%)" ([#5619](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5619) / Discord, 2026-05-13). So even in the worst case MuonH is net-positive in wall-clock, and *this* — not any loss extrapolation — is the decision-relevant argument. On TPU the measured overhead is negligible: at d2560 on v4-1024, **1.49M tok/s with MuonH vs 1.51M tok/s with AdamH** (Larry Dial, 2026-06-23).

**(b) Projected, whole-recipe compute-equivalent speedup — the "~2.12×".** The oft-quoted "~2.12× equal-throughput compute-equivalent speedup at every isoFLOP budget" is a **scaling-law projection, not a measured whole-recipe result, and it originates in [#6074](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6074), not #6153** (ClassicLarry, 2026-05-31 17:16). It comes from a pinned-asymptote fit to the four May-Recipe isoFLOP optima: all-4-budget fit `loss(C)=1.6+81.48·C^−0.0922`; **drop-1e18 fit `loss(C)=1.6+88.90·C^−0.0941`** — *"same α as v16 README curve → uniform 2.12× equal-TPS speedup at every budget."* The uniformity is a **construction artifact**: the drop-1e18 exponent (0.0941) is pinned identical to the v16 baseline, so the compute ratio is constant *by construction*. Its author immediately caveats the dropped anchor: *"Currently I am not trusting the 1e18 data point… Muon does disproportionately well at the very small size with only 1pct warmup. Dropping 1e18 from the fit"* (17:19). The **measured** per-budget optima are *not* uniform — 1e18 2.26×, 3e18 2.12×, 1e19 2.09×, 3e19 2.12× — and each is a measured May-Recipe optimum vs the v16 *fit*, never a measured v16 head-to-head. It also credits the **whole May Recipe** (QB routing, GatedNorm, XSA, half-RoPE, split gate/up, etc.), not Muon alone. [PR #6153](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/6153) *restates* the drop-1e18 fit as the on-main compute-optimal reference; it is not the origin.

**The gate-2 crossover, and how it was retired.** The [#5596](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5596) gate-2 close-out is explicitly a **"mixed verdict"**: MuonH's OOD-Paloma exponent came in *shallow* (α=0.0906 vs baseline 0.0941), the fitted curves **cross near C≈1.65e21**, and the strict 1e23 projection had MuonH **losing** (+0.0106) — so #5596 was left **OPEN**, not adopted. That crossover was diagnosed as a **1%-warmup artifact** and retired by replacement, not by a like-for-like refit: (i) the no-warmup re-run flipped it to a **PASS at both 1e21 and 1e23** (WhenWen, [#5619](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5619), 2026-05-13: "the noise is pretty large so the crossover is not so worrying"), and (ii) the governing #6074 May-Recipe drop-1e18 fit recovers the baseline exponent → **no crossover** (2.213 vs 2.252 at 1e23). *Honest residual:* every fit still shows MuonH's Paloma exponent marginally shallow, and the ~1.2e23 hero run is a real ~3–4-order extrapolation above the largest measured budget (≤3e19); the team's own words are that "the 1e23 prediction is not super meaningful." The run was shipped on the **measured wall-clock win + 1–2% overhead**, which holds regardless of which loss fit you believe.

## 3. Where the current approach actually stands (live run status — the most-cited result of the latest week)

As of the latest corpus week (weekly summary 2026-07-06 → 07-12), the run is **not static**:

- **Main line, live.** The `…_muon_resume15k_v2_10T` run is `state=running`; batch was doubled to 67.1M tokens on the 2026-07-01 resume, lifting **MFU from ~13.5% to ~18.6%** on v4-2048. The summary places it near **step 39,000 (~2.114T tokens, ~21% of 10.07T)**; the frozen W&B head shows the main line has since continued to **_step ≈ 54,922 / ~3.18T tokens** (eval Paloma macro_loss 2.373 at seqlen 8192, pre-cooldown). Completion is targeted **~August 30**. (Raw `total_tokens` counters are known-unreliable across restarts, [#6482](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6482); lean on `_step`.)
- **Topology slowdown, understood but not fixed.** A crash at **step 31,962** + Iris restart landed a worse TPU topology and permanently slowed steps **26.1 s → 30.0 s (+15%)**; David Hall traced it to topology and added logging (not reverted in-corpus).
- **First intermediate (2T) cooldown — CLOSED** ([#6811](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6811)). Forked from step 39,000, a 3,150-step LR decay (bs1024 × seq 65,536 = 67.1M tok/step, ~211B decay tokens, phase-2 data mix, context stretched 8k→65k) finished at step 42,149 at **Paloma macro_loss 2.2772 / bpb 0.8242**. The **preregistered stage-1 target was 2.269** — but that target is measured at **seqlen 8,192, 1,024 seqs, pre-cooldown, 80%-mark, ~24% peak LR** ([#6044](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6044), 2026-06-27), whereas the cut **fully decays LR, uses the phase-2 mix, and evaluates at 8× context**. So the 2.2772 ≈ 2.269 near-match is a **strongly favorable early signal, deliberately NOT apples-to-apples**, and does not retire the 8T preregistration. The checkpoint was published to CoreWeave S3 + a v6e inference zone (Romain Yon) so mid-training/SFT/RL/inference can start early. A **second cooldown at 5T is planned for early August**.
- **The one genuinely open optimizer decision.** With #6811 closed, Larry Dial (2026-07-11) opened questions on the eight cooldown checkpoints: checkpoint **averaging (model soup)**, and — most relevant here — *"We used MuonH for training. How much does swapping to Adam hurt/help? How much does maintaining the optimizer state help?"* Unanswered in-corpus; it is a live discussion for the mid-training continuation, not a decision.

## 4. How we ended up here (timeline)

1. **March — question posed.** "Is Muon a net quality win, loss, or wash on the MoE path?" Muon had only been proven on dense models ([#4034](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4034), dlwh = David Hall, 2026-03-23).
2. **April — bake-off.** An AdamH / Muon / MuonH gate-1 comparison spun off a follow-up to (a) define the MuonH heuristic, (b) A/B against AdamH-2×, and (c) run a Vizier Muon LR/beta search ([#5167](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5167), pc0618). Verdict, 2026-06-06: *"Closing, we went with MuonH here."*
3. **May — swap validated and tuned.** MuonH replacing AdamH on matrices passed gate-1 (d512 macro_loss **3.7542 vs v16 AdamH 3.8104, −0.056**) and gate-2 (**"mixed verdict"**, crossover projected) ([#5596](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5596), WhenWen = Kaiyue Wen); the crossover was re-checked and cleared no-warmup ([#5619](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5619)). Routing ablations settled which non-matrix leaves go where ([#5645](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5645), [#5719](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5719)).
4. **Late May / June — LR refit, isoFLOPs, integration.** The MuonH LR law was refit for the May Recipe ([#5951](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5951)); compute-optimal isoFLOP optima established with the drop-1e18 fit ([#6074](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6074)); the recipe merged to `main` in [PR #6153](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/6153) (2026-06-04). [#4034](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4034) closed *"Integrated MuonH"* (2026-06-06).
5. **June/July — into the hero run.** The recipe carries into the 67B-A2B / 10T-token Grug MoE run ([#6044](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6044)); run names carry the `_muon_` tag. The June→TPU restaging is tracked in [#6704](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6704) (ihodes = Isaac Hodes, a planning issue opened 2026-06-26 *after* the switch), with intermediate cooldowns under [#6811](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6811).

## 5. The hardware split — why the TPU run keeps Muon and the GPU track drops it

This is a **platform split, not a "we fled GPU because of Muon" story.**

**GPU: Muon is a large fixed tax.** On a single 8×H100 node (d2560), the MuonH path ran at **4.75% MFU / 21,962 tok/s / 1.492 s per step**, versus **16.19% MFU / 74,848 tok/s / 0.438 s/step** for the same shape with **SGD** — where SGD is a *cost-isolation control, not a real optimizer option* — because Newton-Schulz ran **one matrix at a time** ([#6493](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6493), dlwh, 2026-06-18; its duplicate [#6492](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6492) was closed same-day). **Crucially, the grouped/stacked amortization did NOT succeed:** the grouped/padded NS path (May143) *did not improve throughput* (tied/marginally worse than one-at-a-time); only a later `stack_batch_4d_sharded` 4D layout was "the first concrete better layout candidate," and it won **only in the isolated update micro-benchmark**, never in a real train profile. The close-out best single-node training number (May214, packed-bank cap-8) was ~6.9 MFU / ~32k tok/s / ~1.02 s — still **~2.34× slower than the SGD control** — and dlwh's terminal verdict was that the residual is an unhideable expert-weight-sized grouped→FSDP restore all-gather that "does not scale away with node count," so "we will need to embrace PP [pipeline parallelism]." David Hall's roofline dashboard measured Muon at **~500 ms of a 1,500 ms** H100 step ([#6573](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6573)); Russell Power's PP work put the FSDP Muon tax at **+0.92 s/step (~60% throughput hit)**, and PP ([#6532](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6532)/#6534) *could not beat FSDP on throughput* and was parked. On the 2026-06-26 OA GPU meeting, with GPU at **~19.9% MFU on 4 nodes with SGD/Adam** ([#6693](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6693)), the call was to **shelve Muon on GPU** ("a ~10% step-count gain isn't worth that interconnect cost"). Larry's later GPU MFU Learning Path itemized real Muon's Newton-Schulz as **the single biggest give-back at ~3.7 MFU points** ([#6979](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6979), via the 2026-07-06 weekly summary; the issue body is not in the frozen corpus).

**TPU: Muon is essentially free.** On the *same* Grug MoE MuonH d2560 path, XLA hides the Newton-Schulz cost — optimizer NS + bookkeeping is **~206 ms, ~0.9% of a ~22 s step** on v4-1024 (BS=4096, seq=8192), and MuonH matches AdamH throughput (1.49M vs 1.51M tok/s). This is *why* the hero run keeps MuonH.

**The platform decision was readiness, not a Muon-retreat.** The June 67B run was **originally planned for CoreWeave H100** ("Run is going by ~June 19th [depends on CW working]," [#6044](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6044) def-of-done). It was **re-planned onto TPU because the H100 cluster wasn't ready** — a first-step memory wall plus immature ~19.9% MFU plus Muon's poor FSDP fit — and staged onto the *mature native* TPU platform where Muon happens to be affordable. TPU was **not** chosen for raw MFU superiority (the run holds only ~18.6% MFU; the parallel H100 11B-A1.5B dry-run cleared ~23.8%, [#6716](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6716)) — it was chosen for maturity/stability + Muon-compatibility + cluster availability. Correspondingly, the **GPU/Blackwell track is a *separate* future-readiness workstream that has *dropped* Muon**: the B200/NVL72 runs ([#6706](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6706), [#6710](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6710), [#6689](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6689)) toward the **September ~512B-A16B** run pursue MFU with SGD/Adam + fp8 + fused kernels (QuACK/SonicMoE, [#7012](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/7012)), and Muon on GPU is contingent on pipeline parallelism landing (JaxPP, [#7024](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/7024), "Hopper is back on the menu"). The team is trying to make Muon cheap *on GPU for the future* — not retreating from GPU to TPU for this run.

## 6. Frontier / not adopted

Explored around MuonH but **not** in the current recipe:
- **MuonEq** (lightweight equilibration before orthogonalization): gate-1 **FAIL** at both exponents, closed ([#6066](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6066)).
- **Left-preconditioned Muon / Mudam** (Shampoo-Muon; Ali Jadbabaie "idea 4"): Mudam ~1.165 vs a Muon control 1.167 c4-en bpb at qwen3-130m — research-stage ([#6588](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6588)); related Ali-ideas activation-aware / gain-gated Muon are research-stage too ([#6538](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6538), [#6506](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6506)).
- **Error-aware / Hessian-corrected Muon speedrun** (Kaiyue Wen — the *same person* as the gate owner "WhenWen"): in a single-seed 40-run sweep, Hessian-corrected Muon beat a fresh Muon control at 4 of 5 LRs by a mean **0.000587 c4-en bpb** — "a single observation, not yet a replicated optimizer win" (PR #7118, via the 2026-07-06 weekly summary; not in the frozen issue corpus).

## Bottom line

The current approach is **MuonH — heuristic-LR-scaled, Hyperball-constrained Muon with Newton-Schulz orthogonalization on the matrix parameter group (minus lm-head), GatedNorms included, K/V kept on Muon** — shipped in the May Recipe ([#6153](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/6153)) and training the live **67B-A2B TPU v4-2048 hero run**, which is **~3.2T of 10T tokens in at ~18.6% MFU** with a first 2T cooldown closed at **2.2772 Paloma macro_loss** (a favorable, *not* apples-to-apples read of the 2.269 stage-1 target). We got here via a March→June MoE bake-off ([#4034](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4034) → [#5167](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5167) → [#5596](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5596)), a per-recipe LR refit ([#5951](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5951)), an isoFLOP compute-optimal study ([#6074](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6074), source of the *projected* ~2.12× whole-recipe speedup), and a hardware split that keeps Muon on TPU (free under XLA) while the GPU/Blackwell track drops it ([#6493](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6493), [#6693](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6693)). The load-bearing case for Muon is the **measured 30–40% step-wise speedup against a 1–2% overhead**, not the loss extrapolation. The one open optimizer question is whether to **swap MuonH → Adam** on the intermediate cooldown checkpoints ([#6811](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6811)).

### Why the "H", and why not plain Muon (quantified)

Direct head-to-head for the hyperball wrap: in the GatedNorm-routing ablation ([#5750](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5750), d512), dropping the hyperball (`gn→muon`: NS direction + Keller post-scale, no hyperball) scored **3.7557** Paloma macro vs **3.7471** with it (`gn→muonh`) — no-hyperball is **+0.0086 worse** (cleanest hyperball-value datapoint; within-routing, not a recipe-wide sweep). And the margin that rejected *plain* Muon: gate-1 ([#5167](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5167)) best plain-Muon at d512 was **3.80732 vs AdamH 3.81035** (~−0.003, a wash), whereas MuonH later delivered **3.7542 vs 3.8104 (−0.056)** — plain Muon was a tie, MuonH a real win.
