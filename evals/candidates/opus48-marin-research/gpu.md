# How GPU training is going

**Short version (as of the week of July 6–12, 2026):** GPU training has moved from a side-experiment to a central, well-staffed workstream, because the whole rest of Marin's 2026 plan runs on NVIDIA hardware — a ~120B-A8B run on 2 NVL72 (B200) at the end of July, and the year's biggest hero run (~512B-A16B on 20T tokens) on twelve NVL72 racks landing ~September 15 ([#6710](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6710)). July is explicitly a *preparation* month for that. The state is "genuinely working and improving, but model-FLOPs-utilization (MFU) is still under target on both Hopper and Blackwell, with pipeline parallelism the named fix." The two current hero *pretraining* runs are still on TPU; GPUs are being hardened and tuned to take over.

Note on hardware split throughout: **H100 = Hopper**, **B200/GB200 = Blackwell / NVL72**. Numbers are kept attached to their platform because they are not interchangeable.

## H100 (Hopper): real runs land, MFU ~24%, weak-scaling holds

- **The July H100 hardware-and-loss validation run is live and healthy.** The Marin **11B-A1.5B MoE** (d2048, 24 layers, 64 experts top-4, GQA 4:1; ~1.53B active / 10.6B total) came up on **64×H100 on CoreWeave** and has held **~23.8% MFU** (~1.37M tokens/s, ~1.53 s/step at batch 512 × seq 4096), with completion expected ~July 13–14 ([#6716](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6716)). This is deliberately *not* about the artifact — it is pushed to 500B tokens, well past compute-optimal, "to shake out the GPU training stack and exercise the hardware ahead of the larger Blackwell runs" (Isaac Hodes: "this is more about exercising the hardware instead of delivering an artifact") ([#6716](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6716)). Caveat carried in the source itself: the FLOP counter treats attention as fully quadratic and ignores the 2048-token sliding window, so the reported MFU is *slightly optimistic* ([#6716](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6716)).
- **The GPU path tracks the TPU reference chain on loss.** Two d512 compute-optimal chains on 8×H100 walked Paloma macro-loss down cleanly with each stack change (pure main 3.7103 → +VMAP w_gate fix 3.6954 → +256 experts 3.6359), confirming the GPU path behaves as expected; the residual gap to the TPU chain traces to *data mix* (datakit vs nemotron), not hardware ([#6716](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6716)).
- **A "GPU Upskilling Master Plan" now organizes the effort** ([#6998](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6998), David Hall): four workstreams (an XXB-A2B Hopper target at 20–25% MFU on 128–256 H100; a Blackwell A8B smoketest; MXFP8 on Blackwell; a fused expert-parallel MoE kernel). It names the Hopper obstacles plainly: high sparsity + Adam tax HBM, the MoE block is slow, and Muon carries high overhead at high sparsity — with **pipeline parallelism (PP) as the real fix**.
- **Weak-scaling is essentially flat to 64 chips.** Larry Dial's "GPU MFU Learning Path" ([#6979](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6979)) built the Hopper story from scratch on 8×H100 and found the full trainable MoE `model.py` weak-scales **26.7% MFU at 8 GPUs → 26.5% at 64 H100s** (global batch 1024), because DDP hides the once-per-step gradient all-reduce over the data-center network while keeping the frequent FSDP all-gathers on intra-node NVLink; sharding the *model* across nodes instead costs ~4 points. Dropping total experts 256→64 (still top-4) lets the model fit one node, proposed as the H100 working copy ([#6979](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6979)).
- **Pipeline parallelism is advancing — "Hopper is back on the menu."** JaxPP PP-MoE training now stands up across four 8×H100 nodes, best measured **mean MFU 18.26 (~414k tokens/s)** — ~1.74 points below the 20% bar — after a disciplined week of mostly *negative* A/B results (latency-hiding flag, XLA ring combine, Triton routing, etc. all failed to close the gap) ([#7024](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/7024)). David Hall: "Hopper is back on the menu" ([#7024](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/7024)).

## B200 (Blackwell / NVL72): the gating number — 17.8% vs a 20% bar

- **B200 MFU is at 17.8% single-node and not yet over the bar.** The end-of-month ~120B-A8B run on 2 NVL72 is *gated* on B200 MFU clearing **20%** ([#6706](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6706)). It is not there yet, but the gap is closing: Will Held reproduced the H100 config at 12.5% on 8×B200, found **width is the live lever and batch is dead** (the workload is arithmetic-intensity-starved), then wired Tri Dao's **QuACK SM100 grouped GEMM (the SonicMoE kernel)** into the expert MLPs to lift whole-model 8×B200 MFU to **17.8% at d5120** (14.9% at d2560); the QuACK gated GEMM alone hits ~1,175 TFLOP/s, ~52% of peak ([#7012](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/7012)).
- **17.8% is achieved; 20% is a bar and 25% is a September *target*, not results.** The B200 perf epic ([#6710](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6710)) is separately pushing MFU toward **25% for the September ~512B-A16B run** via the remaining fp8 and kernel/PP paths — that 25% is a goal, not a measurement.
- **fp8 is now a real training path (still off by default).** fp8 ragged-dot ops are threaded through the grug MoE expert MLP with opt-in fp8 over-the-wire collectives (E4M3 fwd / E5M2 bwd on permutation legs only; reductions stay bf16); at the production d2560/E256/K4 shape the full MoE layer measures **1.53× vs bf16** at 2-node EP16 over InfiniBand ([#7079](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/7079)). Defaults stay bf16 — the explicit gate before flipping is loss-curve validation on a real trajectory ([#6699](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6699)). The original "try out fp8 training" issue and the old H100×8 MFU tracker were both closed as done/superseded ([#6048](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6048), [#4302](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4302)).
- **Fused MoE kernels progress on both architectures.** David Hall's Pallas Mosaic-GPU *source-push* MoE now completes the exact EP8 forward+backward through one JAX-native custom VJP ([#6597](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6597)); the Blackwell sibling cleared its 300 TFLOP/s-per-rank forward target (666 useful TFLOP/s steady-state) and is being integrated ([#6933](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6933), [#6970](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6970)).

## A notable correctness catch

Every dense-LM GPU run had been **silently slow**: levanter's GPU default attention backend was NVTE (Transformer Engine), which no marin environment ships, so runs fell back to an unfused reference path that also OOMs at sequence 8192. The fix wires in the already-shipped FlashAttention-4 / CuTe kernels as the GPU default — **3.4× faster** (11.3 vs 38.9 ms/iter on an H100) ([#7013](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/7013), [#7015](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/7015)).

## Infrastructure: the GPU fleet tripled and federation went live

- **RNO2A came online**: Russell Power stood up a second CoreWeave cluster (64 nodes × 8 H100 = **512 GPUs**, pinned warm), tripling the H100 fleet; its first ~1,500-pod soak shook a week of hardening out of the Iris control plane, and a multi-node "bad InfiniBand" hang turned out to be a Levanter tokenizer-staging thread race, not fabric ([#6909](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6909), [#6950](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6950)).
- **Iris cross-cluster federation is live**: marin can now hand *whole jobs* to CoreWeave, with identity/authorization/trust layers, capacity-aware placement (jobs park as `QUEUED_HANDOFF` and drain to peers with free capacity), and a GPU serving job on CoreWeave reachable through the parent's `/proxy` without tunneling ([#7064](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/7064), [#7034](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/7034), [#7043](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/7043), [#7108](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/7108), [#7109](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/7109)).
- Multi-node NCCL bootstrap on CoreWeave (`cw-rno2a`) and a cuDNN-precedence/CUDA-13 canary issue were both root-caused and fixed ([#7018](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/7018), [#6941](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6941), [#6947](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6947)).

## Trajectory (late June → mid July)

The direction is clearly upward. In late June the fp8 lane **beat both of its preregistered bars** — a 1.41× fp8 grouped-GEMM expert MLP ([#6880](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6880)) and 1.438× end-to-end with fp8 on the dispatch/combine wires ([#6911](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6911)) — and the fused source-push MoE kernel hit **218 TFLOP/s per rank** and ~20% MFU in a one-node Hopper trainer smoke ([#6597](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6597)). Since then the work has been about *scaling that out* (multi-node weak-scaling, pipeline parallelism, wiring fp8/QuACK into real runs) and *hardening the fleet* (RNO2A, federation). ([July-6→12 weekly summary](https://mws.oa.dev/summaries/summary-2026-07-06_2026-07-12.html).)

## Bottom line

- **Working:** GPU runs land and are healthy on H100; loss tracks the TPU reference; the fleet has tripled (512-GPU RNO2A) and cross-cluster federation to CoreWeave is live; fp8 and QuACK/SonicMoE kernels are real and gave large single-layer/kernel speedups.
- **Below target, but closing:** MFU is the live blocker — H100 real run ~23.8% (with a slightly-optimistic caveat), H100 PP-MoE best 18.26, **B200 17.8% vs a 20% gating bar**, and a **25% September target still ahead**. Width (not batch) is the B200 lever; pipeline parallelism is the named fix on Hopper.
- **Still open / on TPU:** The end-of-July B200 run's data mix, architecture, and preregistered loss target are undecided ([#7073](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/7073)); the two in-flight hero *pretraining* runs remain on TPU (the 67B-A2B on v4-2048), with GPUs being readied to carry the September hero run.
