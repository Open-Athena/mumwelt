# How we did on the April 2026 milestone

**Short version: the headline goal was met, and met well — but it was the *only* goal cleanly met, and the milestone's own name over-promised the model that was actually delivered.** The milestone was to *kick off* a preregistered large MoE run; it launched on 11 April and, when it finished on 20 May, landed within 1% of a preregistered loss extrapolated from runs 333× smaller. Almost everything else — the MFU target, the canary reliability target, the library goal, the isoflop ablations — either missed its stated bar or slipped past April. **No retrospective was ever written**, so nothing in the corpus scores the milestone as a whole; the assessment below is assembled from per-epic evidence.

## What the milestone was

The April milestone was titled **"Kick-off pre-trained 100B-A13B 1.2T token MoE (pregistered)"**, confirmed verbatim in the epic-creation comment on the infra tracker ([#4256](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4256#issuecomment-4158313540)) and carried as the `Milestone:` line in the weekly summaries for the weeks of [2026-04-06](https://mws.oa.dev/summaries/summary-2026-04-06_2026-04-12.html), [2026-04-13](https://mws.oa.dev/summaries/summary-2026-04-13_2026-04-19.html), and [2026-04-26](https://mws.oa.dev/summaries/summary-2026-04-26_2026-05-02.html). It replaced March's "Kick-off a 32B-A4B 10T token MoE training run…" ([2026-03-30 summary](https://mws.oa.dev/summaries/summary-2026-03-30_2026-04-05.html)) and was itself replaced by "May: Prepping scaling training recipe + data mix + GPU training" from the week of [2026-05-04](https://mws.oa.dev/summaries/summary-2026-05-04_2026-05-10.html).

It was structured as **nine epics** — six infrastructure, split from [#4256](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4256) ("Improving our scalability, reliability and monitoring in anticipation of our 100B training run and potential GPU allocations"), and three modeling, split from [#4266](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4266). Both parent trackers were deliberately closed and removed from the milestone within a day of creation, ihodes explicitly so the parents wouldn't "pollute our completion count" ([#4256](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4256#issuecomment-4159287747)).

## The headline deliverable: kicked off on time, and the prediction held

**Launched.** ClassicLarry, 2026-04-13: *"I went ahead and started the run on Apr 11th on a v4-1024. Sometime this week we can migrate it to v4-2048 with improved ragged all-to-all setup for better MFU."* ([#4697](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4697#issuecomment-4239090396))

**Completed — in May, not April.** ClassicLarry, 2026-05-20: *"Final paloma/macro_loss was 2.234, 1% better than the initial prediction of 2.252."* ([#4697](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4697#issuecomment-4498921338)). Sealed by dlwh on 2026-05-21 with `Final W&B state: finished, global_step=120331`, `eval/macro_loss=2.2333362102508545`, seal tag `grug-moe-1e23-compute-optimal-seal-20260520` ([#4447](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4447#issuecomment-4504592071)). Announced by dlwh in Discord: *"Our compute-optimal 1e23 MoE is finished! Final Paloma macro-average loss was 2.234, less than 1% off from the preregistered 2.252. That prediction came from runs 333x smaller."* ([#general, 2026-05-20](https://discord.com/channels/1354881461060243556/1354881461060243561/1506713859824816269))

This is the milestone's real win, and it is a methodological one: the preregistration held. The prediction came from Larry's L∞-fixed-at-1.6 isoflop fit `macro(C) = 1.6 + 95.182 · C^(-0.0941)` → 2.252 ([#4447](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4447#issuecomment-4225593039)). It also beat a competing forecast — Helw150's VPNLS estimate of *"for the 1e23: 2.295"* ([#4697](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4697#issuecomment-4311029604)). Notably this was a *correction* of April's earlier miss: the preceding 1e22 run came in at 2.432 against a predicted 2.3887, which ClassicLarry traced to *"the asymptote of the 3 point fit above … was -0.1. This is unrealistically low"* ([#3800](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/3800#issuecomment-4203453566)) — a calibration lesson that fed directly into the 1e23 prediction that then held.

**But the name did not match the model.** The milestone promised **100B-A13B on 1.2T tokens**; what shipped was **129.1B total / 15.9B active on ~1.01T tokens**, d5120, 48 layers, 64 experts, K=4, Nemotron mix ([#4697 job summary](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4697#issuecomment-4239090890)). ihodes flagged the token discrepancy mid-flight — *"I think compute optimal = ~1T / 1e12 tokens here?"*, answered *"Yes"* by ClassicLarry ([#4697, 2026-04-17](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4697)). This is a real gap between the milestone as advertised and as delivered, though the delivered model is the compute-optimal one for the 1e23 budget.

**The run was not smooth.** It required at least four resumes, three of which are recorded as `crashed` W&B runs before `..._resume112662_clip15_20260518_123236` finished. The worst incident, dlwh 2026-05-02: *"Corrupted multihost resume attempts … left only one trustworthy permanent checkpoint, so recovery cost more than 8k steps"* ([#5386](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5386)). The ragged expert-parallel path also exploded on first contact: *"The `moe_1e23_apr` 1e23 MoE smoke explodes on step 0/1 when we exercise ragged expert parallelism at `ep>=8`. `grad/norm/output_proj` jumps from the expected ~0.6 range to 8k-330k."* ([#4746](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4746)), fixed via an A2A offset fix reaching ring/ragged parity at *"about `4e-8`"* relative L2 ([#4697](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4697#issuecomment-4271393606)).

## Epic-by-epic scorecard

### Modeling

| Epic | Bar | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| [#4281](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4281) MoE Scaling | 3 goals | **2 of 3.** `[x]` 1e22 isoflop completed, `[x]` 1e23 launched + preregistered, `[ ]` *"Isoflop ablations on key decisions ('Good/Great 10T') up to 1e21 flops"* — still unchecked |
| [#4282](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4282) Agentify | agents run a full architecture-idea loop | **Met, on a marginal result.** Closed for April |
| [#4283](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4283) MoE MFU at scale | 25–30% MFU | **Missed; slipped to June** |

The **stretch goal** — *"1e23 completed (~16 days on v4-2048)"* — slipped: the run took from 11 April to 20 May, and started on v4-1024 rather than v4-2048.

**MFU is the clearest miss.** The target was *"TPU v4: 25%-30% MFU sustained for 100B-A13B on v4-1024"*, with dlwh candidly noting *"Made these numbers up because i don't have any TPU v4 MOE intuition yet"* ([#4300](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4300)). Achieved, all on TPU v4: **14.23%** early on v4-1024 with EP4 ring dispatch ([#4697](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4697#issuecomment-4239090890)), rising to **16.36%** on v4-2048 with EP8 ragged at step 45,337 ([#4697, 2026-04-20](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4697#issuecomment-4283224985)) — dlwh described the migration as *"~20% better MFU (relative)"* ([#moe, 2026-04-19](https://discord.com/channels/1354881461060243556/1365044508546568372/1495530492001386496)). The highest sourced figure for this run is 16.36%, well short of 25–30%. dlwh closed the target on 2026-05-31 with a two-word *"good enough"* ([#4300](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4300#issuecomment-4586150714)) — an acceptance, not an attainment. The H100 half ([#4302](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4302)) slipped entirely and was repurposed as the *June* milestone tracker on 2026-06-05.

The agentify win deserves a caveat. ihodes closed it citing *"the first cleanly Gate-2-promotable architecture change (AdamH-on-embed, #5184)"* ([#4282](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4282#issuecomment-4365370477)), but the experiment's own verdict was softer: *"**Marginal PASS gate 2.** Speedup > 1 at all scales but fading (1.05→1.01)… The benefit of AdamH on embeddings is real but small, and diminishes at larger scales"* ([#5184](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5184)). Both readings are defensible; the close-out is the more generous one.

**Ray sunset is the infra success.** ihodes, 2026-05-03: *"Ray is functionally gone after the late-April delete spree (#5028, #5031, #5076, #5087, #5089, #5131, #5132, #5137, #5138, #5140 and friends)"*, with yonromai verifying the next day: *"Ray is code-complete from Marin's side, with one live-infra tail called out below"* — that tail being that *"`marin-big-run` is still the last Ray cluster because the 1e23 run is still using it"* ([#4269](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4269#issuecomment-4374861480)). So the milestone's own headline run was the last thing keeping Ray alive.

**Capacity landed materially:** PR [#4379](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/4379) (2026-04-03) replaced the preemptible boolean with a `CapacityType` enum (`PREEMPTIBLE, ON_DEMAND, RESERVED`) plus the GCP queued-resource API, and [#4662](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/4662) expanded the v4-reserved pool to 32–4096 (2026-04-12). The stretch goal of good queuing/preemption was not reached — [#3487](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/3487) stayed open with rjpower's critique that blocking is *"done crudely, at the worker level: reserved jobs take over an entire worker"*.

**Marin-as-a-library was oversold as complete.** rjpower's 2026-04-15 update is real progress — *"We now have 2 demo examples available via marin-experiments/ … These should be good enough for a 'getting started' by volunteers"* ([#4271](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4271#issuecomment-4256321821)) — but the stated bar (an external project pip-installing and importing marin without Iris/Ray) is not evidenced, and every checkbox including all five sub-issues remains unchecked. Worth correcting a claim that circulated in the weekly summary: the April nightly publishing went to **GitHub Releases**, not PyPI ([PR #4612](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/4612), 2026-04-10, *"publishes one GitHub Release per package"*). PyPI came in May ([#5570](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/5570), [#5682](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/5682), [#5870](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5870)) and was still broken then — *"marin-finelog 0.99 missing from PyPI breaks `uv add marin-zephyr`"* ([#5867](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5867), 2026-05-20).

**Canary reliability: no evidence the 90% bar was hit.** All four of [#4270](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4270)'s measurable criteria remain unchecked, including *"Rolling 30-day canary pass rate is >= 90%"* and the sub-85% alerting criterion; the checked items are tracking/dashboard/triage work, not the outcome. No measured pass-rate percentage exists as text in the corpus. April was instead heavy canary firefighting ([#4362](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4362), [#4624](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4624), [#4654](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4654), [#4739](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4739), [#4831](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4831)), and the one hard number anywhere is from *after* the milestone: rjpower, 2026-06-08 — *"The daily TPU canary (`marin-canary-ferry.yaml`) was red for 8 consecutive scheduled runs (Jun 1–8)"* ([PR #6289](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/6289)).

**Observability closed on a technicality.** ihodes proposed closing #4273 on 2026-05-03 — *"The April scope landed end-to-end — log/stats service split (#5212, #5290, #5370), endpoint proxy + IAP (#5336, #5349), BackgroundTracker (#5332)"* — but that claim is loose: #5290 was still unmerged that day, and #5336, #5349, #5370 all landed on 2026-05-01, i.e. in May. Nobody answered the question, and the epic sat for 33 days until ihodes closed it on 2026-06-05 with *"Closed, as this was a catch-all."* ([#4273](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4273#issuecomment-4634993317))

## Process: nobody actually graded it

There is **no April retrospective, post-mortem, or milestone close-out document in the corpus**. What exists is a burst of per-epic comments by ihodes all timestamped **2026-05-03 04:16**, each arguing "closeable for April" — covering [#4269](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4269#issuecomment-4365370292), [#4273](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4273#issuecomment-4365370410), [#4282](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4282#issuecomment-4365370477), and [#3192](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/3192#issuecomment-4365370520). That sweep touched only 3 of the 9 milestone epics; [#4268](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4268), [#4270](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4270), [#4271](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4271), and [#4272](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4272) received no close-out signal at all.

The tracking apparatus itself came in for tacit criticism. Three epics were ultimately retired as "catch-alls" in a single 2026-06-05 sweep ([#4273](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4273#issuecomment-4634993317), [#5369](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5369)), and Helw150 said of the synthetic-data tracker: *"We should close and split up into something with good definitions of done. Sorry for that."* ([#3192](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/3192)). Meanwhile the June milestone tracker is a *repurposed April issue* — dlwh, 2026-06-05: *"Done: this issue is now the June milestone tracker, replacing #6049."* ([#4302](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4302#issuecomment-4636450329)) — which suggests milestone boundaries functioned more as bookkeeping than as assessed checkpoints.

## Verdict, with the caveats attached

- **The one thing the milestone was named for, it did**: the run kicked off in April, preregistered, and landed within 1% of prediction. That is a genuine scientific result, not a process artifact.
- **Everything with a *number* attached to it missed**: 25–30% MFU → ~16%; ≥90% canary → not demonstrated; 1.2T tokens → 1.01T; 100B-A13B → 129B-A16B; "~16 days" → ~39 days.
- **Roughly 2–3 of 9 epics closed cleanly**; the rest were partial, unassessed, or administratively closed weeks-to-months later.

**Explicit gaps** — things the corpus does *not* support, which should not be inferred: there is no numeric milestone completion count (e.g. "7/9") anywhere; the corpus stores issue and comment text, not GitHub milestone open/closed state, so the milestone object's own status is unknown; checkbox states reflect the 2026-07 freeze rather than the end-of-April state; and no measured canary pass-rate percentage exists for April in any form. There is also no capacity-factor-corrected GitHub-perplexity number for the 1e23 run, despite Larry predicting *"1e23 EP=8 should show bigger gap"* ([#moe, 2026-04-20](https://discord.com/channels/1354881461060243556/1365044508546568372/1495585893459820665)) — so the sealed 2.2333 may be slightly pessimistic.
