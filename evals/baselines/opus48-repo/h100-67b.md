# Bringing the 67B-A2B up on H100s

## Short answer

The 67B-A2B (the "June" Grug MoE: `d=2560`, 26 layers, 256 experts, top-k 4 — ~67B total / ~2B active) **never ran at its full 256-expert shape on H100s** — that shape is memory-negative on a Hopper node and OOMs before the first step. The hero pre-training/cooldown actually landed on **TPUs** ([#6704](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6704), [#6044](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6044), [#6811](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6811)). The H100 effort was a derisking / GPU-upskilling / perf-tuning exercise ([#6998](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6998) "GPU Upskilling Master Plan"), which pivoted to a reduced **64-expert "working copy"** that *does* fit a single node, and drove that to a best-of **~28% MFU** on 8×H100 with clean weak-scaling to 64×H100. The whole line was then closed out and moved to B200/Blackwell.

## Framing / goal

- The GPU Upskilling Master Plan ([#6998](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6998)) set the "XXB-A2B on Hopper" work stream: **A2B MFU ≥ 20–25% on ~128–256 Hoppers**. It named the three known blockers up front: high sparsity + Adam over-taxes HBM (forces too much FSDP/EP or too-small batch), the MoE block is slow, and Muon has high overhead on Hopper at high sparsity. It notes the "real fix" is pipeline parallelism, which they punted on.
- The derisking question ([#4302](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4302), [#4312](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4312)): can Grug/Levanter reach acceptable MoE throughput on NVIDIA shapes (within ~10% of Megatron/TE BF16), or do we have to switch stacks? Target was to *not* have to move to Megatron.
- Earlier milestone ([#5356](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5356), closed): just get a June A2B MoE to train ~1k steps on 2+ GPU hosts on CoreWeave — reported "ran multinode, mostly stable, next: perf work."

## What didn't work: the full 256-expert 67B shape ([#6367](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6367))

Issue [#6367](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6367) ("Grug MoE d2560 H100 MFU") tracked making the full `d2560 / 26L / 256-expert / top-k4` shape fast on CoreWeave H100s at ≥20% MFU. It **never got a ≥20% MFU claim.** The full-depth, batch-256, seq-4096 shape is **memory-negative** — it OOMs before step 0 (~74 GiB allocation request) under every variant tried:

- fp32 live params, bf16-live-params-with-fp32-master, Pallas CE, and `recompute_all` all OOM'd pre-step (74.75 / 74.42 / 74.41 / 72.03 GiB requests).
- Diagnostics localized it: full-depth B128 fits, one-layer B256 fits, and full-depth B256 at `seq_len=2048` fits — proving the blocker is **sequence/token-scaled activation/remat liveness**, not static param/optimizer storage.
- Repeated 128-device "Initialize clique" rendezvous stalls forced dropping from 16 nodes (128 GPUs) to 8 nodes (64 GPUs), and reducing global batch (B256→B128→B64) rather than adding microbatching.
- Also found invalid: mixed ring-EP + `model_axis>1` (collective/`kCollectivePermute` rendezvous failures) — production topology kept at `expert_axis=8, model_axis=1, moe=ring`.

Conclusion of #6367: the original full shape stays memory-negative at seq4096; the path was to profile a reduced B64 run and decide the next bottleneck. In practice the effort pivoted (see below).

## What worked: the reduced 64-expert "working copy" ([#6979](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6979))

Issue [#6979](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6979) ("GPU MFU Learning Path", closed) is the H100 bring-up story. Key move: **drop total experts 256 → 64 (top-4)** so the full model fits a single 8×H100 node, and treat "pick-4-from-64" as the H100 working copy, scaling total experts only as hardware allows. Progression (branch `dial_h100_dev`, `cw-us-east-02a`):

**Component MFU milestones (single 8×H100 node):**
| Milestone | Config | Best MFU |
|---|---|---|
| lm_head-only (CE benchmark) | 0.43B | 50.3% |
| Full dense | d2560/26L, 2.70B | 56.2% |
| Alternating MoE (13 MoE layers, e64 top-6) | 10.2B | 35.5% |

**The "climb then give-back" arc** (d2560/64E top-4): throughput-tuning peaked at **34.1%** MFU (bf16, zero-init probe — batch 32→128 was the biggest lever at +22.8pt; backend scatter/ring/sonic spread only ~1.5pt, sonic best). Then each *correctness* feature needed for a real trainable model was itemized as a give-back down to **26.7% MFU** for the full `model.py`:
- Real Muon Newton-Schulz cost **−3.7pt** (34.4% → 30.7%) — the single biggest give-back (orthogonalization + expert all-to-all reshard).
- Each norm (RMSNorm, QK-norm, GatedNorm) ~0.4–1pt; MuonH + real init another chunk.

**Multinode scaling worked cleanly:** DDP/replica weak-scaling stayed **flat at ~26.5% MFU from 1→8 nodes (up to 64 H100s at global batch 1024)** — the cross-node grad all-reduce over the DCN is fully hidden, FSDP all-gathers stay on intra-node NVLink. (A found bug: a step-10 OOM was not memory but the `watch` callback unstacking all 26 scanned layers for per-parameter norms; fixed with `SCALE_WATCH=0`.) FSDP beat expert-parallel at these sizes; FSDP-across-nodes costs ~4pt vs replica because per-layer all-gathers traverse the slower DCN.

## Final / best MFU numbers

On the refined shape `d2048 / 24L / 64E top-4 + 1 shared / seq4096`, single 8×H100 node ([#6979](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6979)):

- **Best GQA: 28.16% MFU, 203k tok/s (25.4k/GPU)** — pure FSDP (EP=1, data=8), stacked `lax.scan` layers, ring MoE, `gpu_fa4_cute` (FA4-CUTLASS) attention, `recompute_all`, batch 256, MuonH.
- **Best MLA: 21.51% MFU, 155k tok/s** — after an MLA attention-param data-sharding fix that moved the batch ceiling 32→256 (14.7% → 21.5%). MLA also required kernel work to support its asymmetric qk=192/v=128 MHA in the FA4 CuTe kernel.
- Honest hardware-MFU (accounting for causal + sliding-window layers): GQA ≈ **24.4%**, MLA ≈ **24.8%**. tok/s is formula-independent.
- Independent reproduction on NVIDIA's NGC stack (Reno H100s) matched: **26.74% MFU / 210k tok/s** for the GQA baseline.

**Stacked throughput levers (final result):** te_grouped MoE (+3.3%, TE cuBLAS grouped-GEMM for the two expert GEMMs) + grad-only "loss-free" chunked CE (+2.0%) + distributed non-expert Muon NS (+0.7%) = **+6.3% e2e, loss-neutral** → **227k tok/s, ~26.7% true MFU (31.5% on the legacy `lm_flops_per_token` dashboard counter)** on the GQA d2048 baseline. Weak-scales flat and linear to 64×H100.

## NVIDIA fast-EP paths evaluated ([#6979](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6979))

- **`jax.ragged_dot` cuDNN fusion — dead for training.** Forward fuses on Hopper (cuDNN 9.23 SM90 engine; not on Blackwell/SM100), but the backward OOMs (`haliax.ragged_dot` has no `custom_vjp` → dense per-expert wgrad GEMM ~128 GiB). Grug's Triton `sonic` path sidesteps this.
- **DeepEP — dead.** Builds, but its side-effecting FFI can't be remated (`FfiEffect` under `recompute_all`) and the un-remated dispatch buffer OOMs at 2.34 TiB.
- **NCCL_EP (TransformerEngine 2.17, built from source) — works.** Functional/differentiable FFI survives `jax.checkpoint`, bounded recv capacity. The usable fast-EP path once a model outgrows FSDP; needs the multi-controller / one-process-per-GPU execution model (vs grug's single-controller `shard_map`).
- **`te_grouped` — works** as a local expert-GEMM drop-in (+3.3% e2e), also requiring one-GPU-per-process + a remat boundary to avoid a >74-min compile blow-up.

## Bottom line

- The full **67B (256-expert) shape was never brought up on H100** — memory-negative on Hopper; the real hero run went to TPUs ([#6704](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6704)).
- The H100 line succeeded as a **perf/upskilling** effort on a reduced **64-expert working copy**, hitting the #6998 target band: **~28% MFU (≈24–27% honest) on 8×H100, flat weak-scaling to 64×H100**, with the NVIDIA fast-EP landscape mapped for when models outgrow FSDP.
- Biggest levers up: batch size × backend (sonic) and stacked-scan layers. Biggest correctness costs: real Muon NS (−3.7pt). Remaining bottleneck flagged for future work: a faster EP kernel.
- [#6979](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6979) was **closed 2026-07-16 — "Closing, moving testing to B200."**
