# Our current Muon approach, and how we got here

## Bottom line

Our production optimizer for matrix-shaped parameters is **MuonH** — Muon (Newton–Schulz orthogonalization of the Nesterov-momentum gradient) wrapped in a Frobenius **"hyperball"** norm-preservation step, plus an **"H" scaling heuristic** (a model-size/token/batch-aware LR schedule analogous to our AdamH heuristic). It was ported onto the main MoE template as part of the **May Recipe** in [#6153](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/6153) — `GrugMoeMuonHConfig`, registered `grug_moe_muonh_v1` — replacing AdamH on the weight-matrix + GatedNorm group. This is the recipe feeding the **67B-A2B 10T MoE hero run** ([#6044](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6044)) and its July 2T intermediate cooldown ([#6811](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6811)).

**Parameter routing** ([#5763](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5763), [#6153](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/6153)):
- **MuonH** on all weight matrices (attention, router/MoE experts, shared MLP) **and** on GatedNorm routing weights (`*.gated_norm.w_*`)
- **AdamH** on the token embedding (`adamh_embed`) and `output_proj`/lm-head
- plain **Adam** on router / router-bias / attn-gate / norm vectors and scalars

It ships with **1% warmup, no gradient clipping (`max_grad_norm=None`), and router/final-logit z-loss disabled**. The LR comes from a refit heuristic (`heuristic_v2`): `muonh_lr = 18.31 · tokens^-0.395 · dim^-0.150 · √B` ([#6153](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/6153)).

**How we ended up here.** The through‑line is the "Great 10T" gate, which insisted on *measuring* Muon on MoE rather than importing dense‑model intuition — split into a throughput question ([#4033](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4033)) and a quality question ([#4034](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4034)). Plain, tuned Muon was **not** a clean win: a gate‑1 Vizier LR/beta search only slightly beat AdamH at d512, *lost* at d768, and AdamH‑at‑2×‑batch also underperformed — so the team explicitly chose to carry forward **MuonH** rather than plain Muon or AdamH‑2× ([#5167](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5167)). The decisive positive evidence was the MuonH matrix‑swap ([#5596](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5596), PR #5597): across the measured 2.19e17–2.83e19 FLOP range it gave lower macro loss, ~1.0–2.6% higher throughput, and a 1.19×–1.33× wall‑clock effective speedup. Warmup ablations then showed **no‑warmup / 1%‑warmup beats 10% warmup** ([#5619](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5619)), and the consolidated "GN→MuonH + 1% warmup + no grad‑clip" variant was adopted as the comparison baseline because it won strongly at d512 and was close enough at d768/d1024 ([#5763](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5763)). May Recipe then folded all of this into the template ([#6153](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/6153)), reporting the same scaling‑law exponent as the v16 baseline and ~2.12× equal‑TPS compute‑equivalent speedup.

**Negative / inconclusive branches along the way.** Several nearby ideas were tried and *not* promoted: paired‑experts on `w_down` was neutral at every scale ([#4034](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4034) catalogue), AuroraH expert‑I/O variants gained quality but failed the wall‑clock gates ([#4033](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4033) catalogue), a MuonHR (Riemannian/tangent‑constrained Muon) comparison was scoped but its PR was closed unmerged so no result exists ([#4110](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/4110)), and a ported HybridMuon showed only that its `opt_a` blend beats `opt_f` — an exploratory dense speedrun, not adopted ([#6119](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6119)). The main *live* successor being probed is **NorMuonH** (output‑axis adaptive normalization inside the hyperball), which passes gate 1 tied‑or‑slightly‑better than MuonH but has not yet displaced it ([#5598](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5598)).

**Active research frontier (not yet promoted).** Beyond NorMuonH, a wide bench of Muon variants is under active development on top of the Muon/MuonH base — mostly a family of "Ali Jadbabaie idea" optimizer PRs plus Shampoo/Muon hybrids:
- **Gain-gated Muon** (idea 2, [#6506](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/6506)): SVD interpolation between Muon and a square-root/HJB policy.
- **Activation-aware Muon** (idea 3, [#6538](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/6538)): whiten by the input-activation second moment `Σ=AAᵀ` (KFAC "A"-factor); requires forward capture.
- **Left-preconditioned Muon** (idea 4, [#6588](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/6588)): `U=ρ·H^{-1/2}·polar(H^{-1/2}M)`, `H=EMA(GGᵀ)`; a pure optimizer with no trainer/model changes.
- **Curvature-corrected Muon** ([#6634](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/6634)): a Stiefel-manifold trust-region step with one-sided Shampoo curvature, built on the MuonH hyperball base.
- **Mudam / Shampoo-Muon** ([#6589](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/6589)): one-sided Shampoo preconditioner composed with Muon orthogonalization.
- **PRISM** ([#6301](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/6301)) and an **error-aware Muon** speedrun sweep ([#7118](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/7118)) round out the search. None has displaced MuonH as the default.

**The current bottleneck.** Two things. (1) A **long‑horizon scaling‑law crossover**: the four‑point MuonH fit has a slightly *shallower* alpha than the AdamH baseline, so despite winning across the whole measured range it *ties* around ~1e21 FLOPs and projects ~0.011 *worse* at the strict 1e23 target ([#5596](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/5596)). The stated resolution is to add a higher‑compute MuonH point or refit the baseline on the same recipe before treating MuonH as the settled long‑horizon optimizer — the win is banked in the measured regime the 67B‑A2B runs operate in, but the 10T extrapolation is the open risk. (2) **Muon is expensive on GPU MoE**: single-node H100 profiles show MuonH at ~1.49s/step (21.9k tok/s) vs SGD ~0.44s/step (74.8k tok/s) — Newton–Schulz emits many separate GEMM kernels, and grouped/stacked/padded NS layouts have so far been *negative* once restore/all-gather boundaries are counted ([#6493](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6493)). This feeds the **>20 MFU @128 GPU** workstream ([#6693](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6693)), and we're eyeing OSS **FSDP-Muon** implementations to avoid forcing pipeline parallelism ([#6527](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6527)).
