# Bringing the 67B-A2B shape up on H100s

The H100 effort was a bring-up and performance campaign for the d2560 May-recipe Grug MoE used by the 67B-A2B work, not the successful long 10T training run itself (that long run proceeded on TPU). Its definition of done was a repeatable, profile-bearing short CoreWeave H100 run at ≥20% training MFU ([#6367](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6367)).

## What was tried

The campaign began with the full d2560/L26 shape on up to 32 nodes / 256 H100s. It tested FA4/CuTe attention; fp32 master weights with bf16 live/compute parameters; persistent parameter sharding with temporary gathers for dense components; Pallas versus XLA cross-entropy; several rematerialization policies; replicated versus sharded embedding and LM-head layouts; different expert/model/data axis decompositions; direct global-batch reductions; shorter-sequence diagnostics; expert AdamH in place of the 3D expert optimizer; and topology-preserving one-layer minimizers. Later attempts moved to 128 GPUs and reduced global batches to obtain usable profiles ([#6367](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6367)).

In parallel, the team investigated MuonH because it was disproportionately expensive on H100. A matched single-node A/B measured MuonH5 at 4.75% MFU, 21,962 tokens/s, 1.492 s/step, versus SGD at 16.19%, 74,848 tokens/s, 0.438 s/step. Grouped and padded Newton–Schulz, 4D layouts, packed expert banks, authoritative packed fp32 master state, and different FSDP materialization boundaries were all explored ([#6493](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6493)).

## What worked

The minimizers were useful diagnostically: they separated cluster startup problems from model-shape problems and established that some topology-preserving distributed programs could initialize. Shortening sequence length allowed full-depth jobs to pass the memory gate, showing that activation/liveness at the production sequence length—not merely total tokens per step—was a key constraint. Reducing the true global batch eventually produced profile-bearing full-sequence runs, and the attention/MoE forward-backward path became viable enough to shift attention toward optimizer state and communication ([#6367](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6367)).

The Muon investigation also found real local improvements: grouped/padded and packed-bank layouts made Newton–Schulz computation itself more tractable and validated collective-free or reduced-collective boundaries in focused harnesses. The important limitation is that these were component/harness wins; they did not turn into a clean full-training ≥20% result ([#6493](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6493)).

## What did not work

The first full-shape attempts repeatedly failed before producing an MFU: one terminated before step 0 trying to allocate 60.91 GiB, while others stalled in XLA `Initialize clique`/rendezvous startup across 128–256 devices. Full-depth batch 256 remained over the memory limit even with bf16 live params and aggressive rematerialization. Several profile launches had to be stopped because they remained pre-step with no scalar history; reducing node count and batch was necessary to get evidence ([#6367](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6367)).

Muon grouping alone was not enough. The close-out diagnosis was structural: in an FSDP-like setup, optimizer compute can scale down, but optimizer communication remains large relative to forward/backward and does not disappear with more nodes. Some packed-master paths lowered cleanly yet stayed slow when the full direction construction, state return, or next-loss materialization was included ([#6493](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6493)).

The nearby 90B-total/5.3B-active H100 campaign reinforces the same boundary. On 256 H100s, batching raised MFU from 2.8% to 9.37%, but batch 1536/2048, sequence 4096, PGLE, reshard changes, and microbatching either OOMed or regressed. It closed at 9.37% MFU / 712K tokens/s as the practical pure-FSDP ceiling for that shape, explicitly below the ~25% target ([#6304](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6304)).

## Final MFUs

There is no corpus-supported final ≥20% MFU for the full 67B-A2B H100 configuration. The defensible final picture is:

- **Full production-like d2560 H100 campaign:** the ≥20% goal remained open; many full-scale runs never reached metrics, and later work continued under a >20%-at-128-GPU tracker ([#6367](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6367), [#6693](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6693)).
- **Matched single-node optimizer A/B:** 4.75% MFU with MuonH5 versus 16.19% with SGD on 8×H100; useful diagnosis, not the final distributed recipe ([#6493](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6493)).
- **Closest large-shape pure-FSDP close-out:** 9.37% MFU on the distinct 90B-total/5.3B-active shape over 256 H100s ([#6304](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/6304)).

In short, the team got far enough to identify the real bottlenecks—full-sequence memory/liveness, large-device collective startup, pure-FSDP all-gathers, and Muon optimizer state/communication—but did not achieve the intended ≥20% production-shape H100 result in the frozen period.
